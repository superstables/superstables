# Sample Dollar Whitepaper

Fictional sample document for the Superstables demo.

Version 2.0. Sample Dollar, its issuer and every figure below are invented for demonstration purposes and describe no real stablecoin, company or person.

## Summary

Sample Dollar (SUSD) is a fictional fully reserved stablecoin. Each SUSD is backed by one US dollar of reserves held outside the protocol, and the issuer commits to redeem SUSD for dollars at par for verified partners.

Reserves are held at two custodian banks and consist of cash and short-dated government bills with a maturity of 90 days or less. The reserve is attested monthly by an independent accountant, and the attestation is published within 15 days of month end. The issuer targets a reserve ratio of 100% at all times and holds an additional insurance fund of 1% of supply for operational losses.

SUSD is issued on two test networks at launch. Minting is available only to verified partners, who wire dollars to the issuer and receive SUSD the same business day. Redemptions settle within two business days under normal conditions. Anyone may hold and transfer SUSD; only minting and redemption require verification.

The issuer can freeze an address when required by law or to respond to a confirmed theft. Freezes are logged on chain and reported in the monthly attestation.

Sample Dollar makes no claim to yield for holders. Its purpose is to be a boring, well-reserved unit of account for payments and settlement.

## Tokenomics

SUSD has no fixed supply. Tokens are minted when a verified partner deposits dollars and burned when SUSD is redeemed, so the supply always equals the dollars in reserve. For the first six months the issuer caps supply at 50,000,000 SUSD while operations are proven.

The reserve is invested conservatively. At least 80% of reserves are held in government bills maturing within 90 days, and the remainder is held as cash at the custodian banks. No reserve asset may have a maturity beyond 90 days, and the issuer does not lend, stake or rehypothecate reserves.

Reserve yield belongs to the issuer, not to holders. Of the yield earned, 80% goes to the issuer to fund operations and 20% goes to the insurance fund until that fund reaches its 1% of supply target, after which all yield goes to the issuer.

Fees are simple. Minting is free. Redemption carries a 0.10% fee, with a minimum redemption of 100,000 SUSD. There is no transfer fee on chain beyond the network's own gas.

There is no governance token. Sample Dollar is operated by its issuer, and changes to reserve policy are announced 30 days before they take effect.

## Risks

Sample Dollar is designed to be low risk, but it is not risk free, and this whitepaper describes what could go wrong.

Reserve risk. Government bills can lose value if interest rates move sharply before maturity. Holding bills of 90 days or less limits this, but a forced sale during stress could realise a small loss. The insurance fund exists for this case and covers losses up to 1% of supply.

Custodian risk. Reserves sit at two banks. If a custodian fails, access to that portion of reserves could be delayed. The issuer holds no more than 60% of reserves at any one custodian.

Depeg risk. SUSD trades freely on secondary markets, and its price there can move away from one dollar when redemptions are slow or demand shifts. Only verified partners can redeem at par; other holders depend on partners to arbitrage the price back.

Freeze and censorship risk. The issuer can freeze addresses. A holder whose address is frozen cannot transfer or redeem until the freeze is lifted.

Redemption delay. Under normal conditions redemptions settle within two business days, but the issuer may extend this to five business days during a bank holiday or a liquidity event.

Regulatory risk. Stablecoin rules differ by jurisdiction and change often. The issuer may have to restrict who can hold or use SUSD, or wind down entirely.

Testnet status. Sample Dollar exists only as a demo. Nothing in this document is an offer, a forecast or investment advice.
